# Beyond the Light.

<div class="sub">Permanent architectural lighting, and the system that makes it worth owning.</div>

---

Anyone can put diodes on a roofline. What happens after the install is what separates a light
strip from a lighting system — and that is the whole of what follows.

---

## The house, under your thumb

One tap powers the entire run. One slider takes it from a whisper to full output. The app opens on
a photograph of the actual house, with the current look named above it, so there is never a question
of what is running.

Underneath that simplicity is per-pixel addressing. Not zones of colour — **individual diodes**,
each one independently placed, so the system can trace what the architecture is already doing:
accent every corner, pick out every peak, outline a column run and leave the fascia soft behind it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Full spectrum** | Any colour, plus dedicated warm-to-cool white temperatures |
| **Motion** | A deep library of effects — glow, pulse, chase, twinkle, fade, sparkle, meteor, ripple, plasma, aurora — with per-effect speed and intensity |
| **Channels** | Target one roofline section, accent run, or column on its own |
| **Whites** | Choose a primary and a complement white, or mix your own by RGBW value |

## Downlighting, without the colour

A category of its own, for owners who want the house to look expensive rather than decorated:
Kelvin-temperature white styles, controlled diode spacing, and brightness gradients that read as
architectural downlighting. Plus two variations — a slow galaxy drift, and twinkling stars — for
the nights that want a little more.

## A design library, and a way past it

Nine curated collections ship with the system: architectural white, holidays, seasonal, parties and
events, nature, movies and superheroes, sports, security and alerts, and everything the owner has
built themselves.

When nothing in the library is right, there are two ways forward:

- **Describe it.** Say *"dark green with red accents on the corners, wave moving right to left"* and
  the system composes it, places it against the real roofline map, and puts it on the house.
- **Paint it.** A per-pixel editor with the strip laid out diode by diode. Select by corner, by peak,
  by run, by anchor, or every *n*-th pixel. Paint, erase, undo, and preview live on the house while
  you work.

Anything saved becomes a first-class object: apply it, schedule it, rename it, duplicate it, or hand
it to a sports team as its game-day look.

## It runs itself

Schedules anchor to a clock time or to the property's **actual sunrise and sunset**, computed from
its real coordinates. Set it once; the controller keeps firing whether or not a phone is nearby.

A scheduling assistant watches how the house is actually used and proposes the coming week —
seasonal shifts, holidays, the patterns that keep getting chosen. Nothing is applied without a
confirmation, and everything it creates is an ordinary schedule the owner can edit or delete.

There is also a single switch worth more than it looks: **turn the lights off at sunrise, every
day, whatever is running** — enforced by the controller itself, app closed, phone off, owner asleep.

## Team nights

Add the teams. Choose the look, or let the system build it from the team's own colours. The house
turns over on game day on its own, with configurable lead time and an option to skip games played
entirely in daylight. During the game, scores land on the house as celebrations, and the lights
return to whatever was playing.

Coordinated streets are supported as well: a shared invite code, a named crew, and one continuous
animation scaled across houses with different diode counts so the colour genuinely travels.

## Control from anywhere

At home, commands go straight to the controller over the local network — the fastest path there is.
Away from home, they route automatically through the **Lumina Bridge**, a small device on the home
Wi-Fi. No port forwarding. No dynamic DNS. No router configuration. The handoff between the two is
automatic and invisible.

## And the quiet parts

- Ask in plain language, in the app, and have it done — or just talked through.
- Siri Shortcuts for saved looks, on iPhone.
- Bluetooth provisioning at install, so a controller joins the network without touching a router.
- Automatic discovery of installed controllers on the local network.
- Encrypted account, per-owner data isolation, and household members invited with their own sign-in.
- A simplified two-tab interface for anyone who wants the lights and nothing else.
